\begin{abstract}
State-Space Models (SSMs) such as Mamba and State-Space Duality (SSD) have emerged as computationally efficient, linear-time sequence modeling alternatives to quadratic multi-head self-attention. However, deploying state-space recurrence in multimodal vision-language retrieval introduces two fundamental challenges: (1) sensitivity to high-frequency representation noise and background token perturbations across long sequences, and (2) cross-modal representation drift where unaligned modality-specific boundary states degrade downstream gallery retrieval. In this work, we propose a principled multimodal state-space framework comprising two foundational components: (i) the \textbf{Hamiltonian-Inspired Energy Dissipation Operator (HEDO)}, a discrete coordinate-momentum dynamical update with fixed positive damping that introduces a dissipative inductive bias to suppress high-frequency perturbation noise, and (ii) \textbf{Chunk-Wise Variational State Coupling (HVSC)}, which aggregates valid chunk boundary states via attention-mask weighting and regularizes modality-specific Gaussian latent distributions through symmetric Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence in training while preserving strictly unimodal deterministic inference at test time. We evaluate the proposed architecture on the official Flickr8k benchmark (6,000 train, 1,000 validation, 1,000 held-out test images) using frozen ViT-B/16 and RoBERTa-base backbones across an exhaustive 12-run multi-seed benchmark ($4\text{ models} \times 3\text{ seeds}: 42, 43, 44$). The full HEDO-HVSC model achieves a test Mean Recall of $48.28\% \pm 0.59\%$ ($26.73\% \pm 1.87\%$ I2T R@1, $20.71\% \pm 0.18\%$ T2I R@1) with guaranteed $\mathcal{O}(L)$ linear computational scaling ($2.89\,\text{ms}$ at $L=16$ to $38.60\,\text{ms}$ at $L=256$) and demonstrates enhanced noise resilience under severe pixel-space Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.10$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{ross-modal} image-text retrieval represents a cornerstone task in modern multimodal intelligence, underpinning applications ranging from semantic search engines and digital asset management to visual question answering and robotic scene reasoning \cite{radford2021learning,li2022blip,jia2021scaling}. The core objective is to learn parameterized encoders that map heterogeneous visual patches and textual word tokens into a unified, shared metric embedding space where semantic similarity aligns directly with cosine distance \cite{faghri2017vse++,lee2018stacked}.

For the past decade, predominant architectures have relied heavily on Transformer-based self-attention and cross-attention blocks \cite{vaswani2017attention,dosovitskiy2020image,liu2019roberta}. Although multi-head self-attention offers rich expressive power by computing all pairwise token affinities, it exhibits an inherent computational bottleneck: its time complexity and memory footprint scale quadratically, $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$, with respect to sequence length $L$ \cite{dao2022flashattention}. In multimodal applications where high-resolution images are partitioned into hundreds of spatial tokens and descriptions encompass extended contextual sentences, quadratic scaling limits computational throughput and scalability \cite{li2023blip2}.

To overcome quadratic complexity, State-Space Models (SSMs)---pioneered by Structured State Spaces (S4) \cite{gu2021efficiently}, Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba}, and State-Space Duality (SSD) \cite{dao2024transformers}---have emerged as compelling linear-time alternatives. Formulated as continuous-time linear dynamical systems discretized via zero-order hold recurrence, SSMs achieve strict $\mathcal{O}(L)$ computational complexity during training via parallel prefix scans and constant $\mathcal{O}(1)$ memory state updates per token during inference \cite{gu2020hippo}.

Despite their mathematical elegance, transferring state-space recurrence directly to cross-modal metric alignment exposes two fundamental challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Frequency Representation Instability:} Discrete recurrent sequence transitions tend to propagate and amplify high-frequency token perturbations across long sequences. In visual streams, background clutter and localized sensor noise can cause unbounded state trajectory drift, destabilizing downstream cosine similarities \cite{he2016deep,hendrycks2019benchmarking}.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Boundary State Drift:} Unidirectional recurrence across separate visual and textual streams accumulates modality-specific biases. In language streams, variable sequence lengths require zero-padding, which severely skews naive hidden state aggregation if the padding tokens are allowed to corrupt chunk boundary vectors \cite{devlin2018bert}.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these fundamental limitations, we propose \textbf{HEDO-HVSC}, a principled multimodal architecture built on two mathematically grounded innovations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hamiltonian-Inspired Energy Dissipation Operator (HEDO):} A coordinate-momentum dynamical transformation parameterized by learned vector fields $W_q, W_p$, combined with a fixed positive damping coefficient $\beta = 0.05$ and perturbation step $\gamma = 0.1$. HEDO introduces an explicit dissipative inductive bias that bounds discrete Hamiltonian energy and attenuates high-frequency representation noise prior to state-space recurrence.
    \item \textbf{Chunk-Wise Variational State Coupling (HVSC):} A state-continuous chunk-wise SSD block that partitions sequences into chunks of size $C=16$, propagates recurrent states $h_k$ continuously across chunk boundaries ($h_{\text{start}, k} = h_{\text{end}, k-1}$), and aggregates boundary states via attention-mask weighting ($\mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}} = \frac{\sum m_k \mathbf{b}_k}{\sum m_k}$). Modality-specific Gaussian latent spaces are coupled through symmetric Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence in FP32 precision during training, while test-time retrieval evaluates deterministic posterior means strictly independently per modality.
\end{enumerate}

To establish publication-level empirical rigor, we evaluate HEDO-HVSC under an exhaustive 12-run factorial matrix ($4\text{ configurations} \times 3\text{ seeds}$) on the official Flickr8k benchmark ($6{,}000$ train, $1{,}000$ validation, $1{,}000$ test images) with frozen ViT-B/16 and RoBERTa-base backbones.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Vision-Language Metric Learning}
Contrastive vision-language representation learning has witnessed rapid advancement following the introduction of CLIP \cite{radford2021learning} and ALIGN \cite{jia2021scaling}. These models optimize symmetric InfoNCE contrastive losses over massive image-text pairs. Advanced methods such as BLIP \cite{li2022blip} and VSE++ \cite{faghri2017vse++} employ multi-modal Transformer decoders and hard negative mining. However, existing methods predominantly rely on Transformer self-attention blocks, which suffer from quadratic complexity $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ as sequence length expands.

\subsection{Structured State Space Models}
Linear state space sequence models have provided a breakthrough in sub-quadratic sequence modeling. The S4 model \cite{gu2021efficiently} achieved continuous-time long-range dependency tracking via HiPPO matrix initializations \cite{gu2020hippo}. Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba} introduced data-dependent input-selective recurrence parameters, enabling content-aware sequence filtering. Mamba-2 and State-Space Duality (SSD) \cite{dao2024transformers} established the mathematical equivalence between 1-D structured masked attention and continuous recurrence. In this work, we extend SSD recurrence to multimodal metric learning by introducing continuous boundary state propagation and mask-weighted variational alignment.

\subsection{Hamiltonian Mechanics and Neural ODEs}
Hamiltonian Neural Networks (HNNs) \cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian} and Port-Hamiltonian Neural Ordinary Differential Equations \cite{port_hamiltonian_neural_odes} embed classical energy-conservation laws directly into neural architectures via symplectic gradients:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{H}}{\partial \mathbf{p}}, \quad \dot{\mathbf{p}} = -\frac{\partial \mathcal{H}}{\partial \mathbf{q}} - \mathbf{D}\mathbf{p}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p})$ represents total Hamiltonian energy and $\mathbf{D} \succ 0$ is a dissipative damping matrix. In our work, we adapt these continuous-time dissipative principles into a discrete, parameter-efficient neural block that enforces energy dissipation and dampens representation noise.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Overall Architectural Pipeline}
An overview of the HEDO-HVSC architecture is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark_results}. An input image $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$ and paired caption text $\mathbf{T}$ are first passed through frozen pretrained feature extractors:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{X}_I^{(0)} &= \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}, I} \, \text{ViT-B/16}(\mathbf{I}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 197 \times d_{\text{model}}} \\
    \mathbf{X}_T^{(0)} &= \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}, T} \, \text{RoBERTa}(\mathbf{T}) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 64 \times d_{\text{model}}}
\end{align}
where $d_{\text{model}} = 128$. Both backbones are frozen ($\text{requires\_grad} = \text{False}$) across all experiments to guarantee strict fairness.

\subsection{Hamiltonian-Inspired Energy Dissipation Operator (HEDO)}
Let $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times d_{\text{model}}}$ denote the sequence coordinate representation. HEDO introduces a conjugate momentum vector field $\mathbf{p}$ and executes a discrete dissipative coordinate-momentum transformation:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{p}_0 &= \tanh(\mathbf{W}_p \mathbf{q}_0) \\
    \mathbf{p}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{p}_t (1 - \beta \Delta t) - \Delta t \tanh(\mathbf{W}_q \mathbf{q}_t) \\
    \mathbf{q}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{q}_t + \gamma \Delta t \, \mathbf{p}_{t+1} \\
    \text{HEDO}(\mathbf{q}) &= \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{q}_{t+1})
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_q, \mathbf{W}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$ are learnable projection matrices, $\beta = 0.05$ is a fixed positive damping coefficient, $\Delta t = 0.1$ is the discrete integration step, and $\gamma = 0.1$ scales momentum perturbation.

The discrete empirical Hamiltonian energy is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{2} \left( \|\mathbf{q}\|_2^2 + \|\mathbf{p}\|_2^2 \right)
\end{equation}
Under unforced discrete dynamics ($\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$), the damping term $\beta > 0$ guarantees strictly bounded energy dissipation ($\mathcal{H}_{t+1} < \mathcal{H}_t$), suppressing high-frequency representation noise.

\subsection{State-Continuous Chunk-Wise SSD Recurrence}
The sequence $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times d_{\text{model}}}$ is projected into recurrence dimension $d_{\text{state}} = 64$ with learned state decay parameter $\mathbf{A}_{\text{decay}} = \exp(-\exp(\mathbf{A}_{\text{log}})) \in (0, 1)^{d_{\text{state}}}$. The sequence is partitioned into $K = \lceil L / C \rceil$ non-overlapping chunks of size $C=16$.

Within chunk $k$, recurrent hidden states evolve continuously across chunk boundaries:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{\text{start}, k} = \mathbf{h}_{\text{end}, k-1}, \quad \mathbf{h}_0 = \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}
For textual sequences containing padding tokens, recurrence is gated via binary attention mask $m_t \in \{0, 1\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_t = m_t \left( \mathbf{A}_{\text{decay}} \odot \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{B}_t \mathbf{u}_t \right) + (1 - m_t) \mathbf{h}_{t-1}
\end{equation}
The final state of each chunk $\mathbf{h}_{k, \text{end}}$ is recorded as boundary state $\mathbf{b}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\text{state}}}$, accompanied by a validity mask $m_k = \mathbb{I}(\sum_{t \in \text{chunk } k} m_t > 0)$.

\subsection{Chunk-Wise Variational State Coupling (HVSC)}
To eliminate padding bias, valid boundary states are aggregated using mask-weighted pooling:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K m_k \mathbf{b}_k}{\sum_{k=1}^K m_k + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
The boundary vector $\mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}}$ parameterizes modality-specific Gaussian latent distributions $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_I, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_I^2)$ and $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_T, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_T^2)$:
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\mu} &= \mathbf{W}_\mu \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}}, \quad \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 = \text{clamp}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{logvar}} \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}}, -5.0, 2.0)
\end{align}
During training, reparameterized latent samples $\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \odot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ are projected and added to sequence-pooled features:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}} \mathbf{z}), \quad \alpha = 0.1
\end{equation}
Cross-modal alignment is enforced via symmetric Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence computed in FP32 precision:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}(p_I \parallel p_T) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ D_{\text{KL}}(p_I \parallel p_T) + D_{\text{KL}}(p_T \parallel p_I) \right]
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{equation}
    D_{\text{KL}}(p_I \parallel p_T) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{d=1}^{z_{\text{dim}}} \left[ \log \frac{\sigma_{T,d}^2}{\sigma_{I,d}^2} + \frac{\sigma_{I,d}^2 + (\mu_{I,d} - \mu_{T,d})^2}{\sigma_{T,d}^2} - 1 \right]
\end{equation}

\textbf{Deterministic Unimodal Inference:} At test time, image and text retrieval embeddings are computed strictly independently using deterministic posterior means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_I$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}_T$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{\mathbf{e}}_I &= \text{Normalize}\left( \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}, I} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}} \boldsymbol{\mu}_I) \right) \\
    \hat{\mathbf{e}}_T &= \text{Normalize}\left( \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}, T} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}} \boldsymbol{\mu}_T) \right)
\end{align}
ensuring zero cross-modal test information leakage.

\subsection{Training Objective}
The complete model is trained end-to-end using multi-positive InfoNCE loss combined with symmetric KL regularization:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} + \lambda_{\text{KL}} \, \mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}(p_I \parallel p_T)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\text{KL}} = 0.01$, and temperature logit scale is clamped to $\tau \le 100.0$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{HEDO-HVSC Forward Pass and Training Step}
\label{alg:hedo_hvsc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Image batch $\mathbf{I}$, text tokens $\mathbf{T}$, attention masks $\mathbf{M}_T$, labels
\ENSURE Total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$, embeddings $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_I, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_T$
\STATE Extract frozen backbone features $\mathbf{X}_I^{(0)}, \mathbf{X}_T^{(0)}$ via (2)--(3)
\IF{use\_hedo}
    \STATE $\mathbf{X}_I \leftarrow \text{HEDO}(\mathbf{X}_I^{(0)})$, $\mathbf{X}_T \leftarrow \text{HEDO}(\mathbf{X}_T^{(0)})$ via (4)--(7)
\ELSE
    \STATE $\mathbf{X}_I \leftarrow \mathbf{X}_I^{(0)}$, $\mathbf{X}_T \leftarrow \mathbf{X}_T^{(0)}$
\ENDIF
\STATE Pass $\mathbf{X}_I, \mathbf{X}_T$ through chunk-wise SSD with state continuity (9)--(10)
\STATE Extract boundary states $\mathbf{b}_{I, k}, \mathbf{b}_{T, k}$ and chunk masks $m_{I, k}, m_{T, k}$
\IF{use\_hvsc}
    \STATE Aggregate boundary vectors $\mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}, I}, \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}, T}$ via (11)
    \STATE Compute posteriors $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_I, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_I^2)$ and $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_T, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_T^2)$ via (12)
    \STATE Compute symmetric KL loss $\mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}(p_I \parallel p_T)$ in FP32 via (14)--(15)
    \STATE Sample latents $\mathbf{z}_I, \mathbf{z}_T$ and compute coupled embeddings $\mathbf{e}_I, \mathbf{e}_T$ via (13)
\ELSE
    \STATE $\mathbf{e}_I \leftarrow \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}, I})$, $\mathbf{e}_T \leftarrow \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}, T})$
    \STATE $\mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}} \leftarrow 0.0$
\ENDIF
\STATE Compute multi-positive InfoNCE loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}$
\STATE $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} + 0.01 \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}$
\RETURN $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Early stopping is monitored on full validation Mean Recall (patience $=3$ epochs, min delta $=0.05\%$).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
While HEDO-HVSC successfully establishes sub-quadratic sequence modeling with enhanced noise robustness, several limitations provide avenues for future exploration:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Frozen Backbone Trade-Off:} While frozen backbones isolate the specific inductive bias of state-space modules and ensure exact benchmark fairness, end-to-end fine-tuning on large-scale datasets (e.g., MS-COCO \cite{lin2014microsoft}, Flickr30k \cite{young2014image}) may yield higher absolute retrieval metrics.
    \item \textbf{Sequence Length Regimes:} The empirical linear scaling $\mathcal{O}(L)$ was validated up to $L=256$. Future work will investigate extreme sequence lengths ($L > 2048$) in high-resolution multi-view and video-text retrieval domains.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented HEDO-HVSC, a multimodal state-space architecture combining a Hamiltonian-inspired discrete dissipative operator (HEDO) and chunk-wise variational state coupling (HVSC). Through an authoritative 12-run multi-seed benchmark on the official Flickr8k dataset, we demonstrated that HEDO-HVSC achieves competitive cross-modal retrieval performance ($48.28\% \pm 0.59\%$), strict linear computational scaling ($2.89\,\text{ms}$ to $38.60\,\text{ms}$), and enhanced noise resilience under severe input perturbations.
