\begin{abstract}
State-Space Models (SSMs) offer sub-quadratic alternatives to self-attention, but deploying state-space recurrence in cross-modal vision-language metric learning raises two practical challenges: sensitivity to representation perturbations and potential misalignment between modality-specific boundary representations. We propose HEDO-HVSC, a multimodal state-space framework with two components: (i) the Hamiltonian-Inspired Energy Dissipation Operator (HEDO), a learned discrete coordinate-momentum transformation with positive damping that introduces a dissipative inductive bias, and (ii) Chunk-Wise Variational State Coupling (HVSC), which performs mask-weighted aggregation of chunk boundary states and regularizes modality-specific Gaussian latents through symmetric Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence during training, while preserving independent, deterministic unimodal inference at test time. In a controlled 12-run factorial benchmark (4 configurations $\times$ 3 seeds) on Flickr8k with frozen ViT-B/16 and RoBERTa-base backbones, the full model achieves a test Mean Recall of $48.28\% \pm 0.59\%$, comparable to a custom SSD-style baseline ($48.25\% \pm 0.47\%$; mean paired delta $+0.03\% \pm 0.20\%$). Corruption stress tests show a mixed robustness profile, with a $+0.50\%$ gain under severe Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.10$), and latency profiling over sequence lengths $L \in [16, 256]$ shows approximately linear empirical scaling.

\keywords{Image-text retrieval \and State-space models \and Hamiltonian-inspired dynamics \and Variational representation learning \and Vision-language alignment}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Cross-modal image-text retrieval is a foundational problem in multimodal machine learning, underpinning image search, visual question answering, and embodied agents~\cite{radford2021learning,li2022blip,jia2021scaling}. The goal is to project visual patch sequences and textual token sequences into a joint metric space where semantic alignment corresponds to vector proximity~\cite{faghri2017vse++,lee2018stacked}. Transformer encoders built on self- and cross-attention are the standard foundation for vision-language models~\cite{vaswani2017attention,dosovitskiy2020image,liu2019roberta}, but their cost grows quadratically, $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$, with sequence length~\cite{dao2022flashattention}, which becomes substantial for high-resolution images and long captions~\cite{li2023blip2,wang2023image}.

Linear-time sequence models such as S4~\cite{gu2021efficiently}, S5~\cite{smith2022simplified}, Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba}, and State-Space Duality (SSD)~\cite{dao2024transformers} mitigate this cost through discretized linear dynamical systems with $\mathcal{O}(L)$ complexity~\cite{gu2020hippo}. Applying state-space recurrence to multimodal metric learning, however, introduces two challenges. First, \emph{representation perturbation sensitivity}: recurrent transitions propagate token variations, so localized noise, clutter, and photometric variation in visual patches can cause hidden-state drift~\cite{he2016deep,hendrycks2019benchmarking}. Second, \emph{boundary representation misalignment}: separate unidirectional encoders may accumulate modality-specific drift, and unweighted pooling lets padding tokens influence final text representations~\cite{devlin2018bert,faghri2017vse++}.

To investigate these challenges, we present HEDO-HVSC. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HEDO}, a learned discrete coordinate-momentum transformation ($\Delta t=0.1$, $\beta=0.05$, $\gamma=0.1$) whose damping term limits momentum amplification prior to sequence recurrence.
    \item \textbf{A state-continuous chunk-wise SSD-style recurrence} ($C=16$, $d_{\text{state}}=64$) in PyTorch that carries hidden states across chunk boundaries without resets.
    \item \textbf{HVSC}, a mask-weighted boundary-state aggregation that parameterizes Gaussian latents regularized by symmetric KL divergence during training, while inference remains strictly unimodal and deterministic.
    \item \textbf{A controlled multi-seed factorial benchmark} (12 runs) on Flickr8k~\cite{hodosh2013framing} with frozen backbones, complemented by corruption testing and latency scaling analysis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Vision-language metric learning.} VSE++~\cite{faghri2017vse++} and SCAN~\cite{lee2018stacked} established hard-negative ranking losses and stacked cross-attention for image-text matching. CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} and ALIGN~\cite{jia2021scaling} showed that dual encoders trained with symmetric InfoNCE on web-scale pairs yield robust representations, and BLIP~\cite{li2022blip} and BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2} added cross-attention decoders for fine-grained reasoning, at quadratic cost in sequence length.

\textbf{Structured state space models.} S4~\cite{gu2021efficiently} formalized continuous-time linear time-invariant systems with HiPPO initializations~\cite{gu2020hippo}; S5~\cite{smith2022simplified} simplified it with parallel scans over diagonal state matrices. Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} made the recurrent parameters input-dependent, and SSD/Mamba-2~\cite{dao2024transformers} established the equivalence between structured masked attention and recurrence. We use a custom PyTorch SSD-style recurrence inspired by this perspective rather than native Mamba-2/SSD CUDA kernels.

\textbf{Hamiltonian and variational models.} Hamiltonian~\cite{greydanus2019hamiltonian} and Lagrangian~\cite{cranmer2020lagrangian} neural networks embed energy conservation into learning, and port-Hamiltonian systems~\cite{massaroli2019port} add positive semi-definite damping. VAEs~\cite{kingma2013auto} and the Variational Information Bottleneck~\cite{alemi2016deep} formalize latent representation learning. HEDO adapts discrete dissipative mechanics into a sequence operator, and HVSC uses Gaussian latents over recurrent boundary states for cross-modal alignment.

\section{Problem Formulation and Background}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}

\textbf{Cross-modal metric learning.} Let $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{I}_i, \mathcal{T}_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ be a dataset where each image $\mathbf{I}_i$ has $M$ captions $\mathcal{T}_i = \{\mathbf{T}_{i,m}\}_{m=1}^M$. We learn encoders $f_{\theta}(\mathbf{I}), g_{\phi}(\mathbf{T}) \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ such that semantic relevance correlates with cosine similarity $s(\mathbf{I}, \mathbf{T}) = f_{\theta}(\mathbf{I})^\top g_{\phi}(\mathbf{T})$.

\textbf{State-space recurrence.} A continuous SSM $\dot{\mathbf{h}}(t) = \mathbf{A}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{B}u(t)$, $y(t) = \mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}(t) + \mathbf{D}u(t)$ discretized with zero-order hold and step $\Delta$ gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_t = \overline{\mathbf{A}} \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \overline{\mathbf{B}} u_t, \qquad y_t = \mathbf{C} \mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{D} u_t,
\end{equation}
with $\overline{\mathbf{A}} = \exp(\Delta \mathbf{A})$. SSD~\cite{dao2024transformers} shows that for scalar-times-identity $\mathbf{A}$ the recurrence admits structured block matrix computation. Our benchmark implementation uses an explicit sequential PyTorch recurrence.

\textbf{Dissipative mechanics.} For coordinates $\mathbf{q}$, momenta $\mathbf{p}$, Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p})$, and damping $\mathbf{D} \succ 0$, dissipative dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{q}} = \nabla_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{H}$, $\dot{\mathbf{p}} = -\nabla_{\mathbf{q}}\mathcal{H} - \mathbf{D}\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{H}$ satisfy $\dot{\mathcal{H}} = -\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{H}^\top \mathbf{D} \nabla_{\mathbf{p}}\mathcal{H} \le 0$. This continuous-time property motivates our construction but does not imply monotonic energy decay for the discrete learned HEDO update; ``energy dissipation'' refers to the damping mechanism and its physical motivation.

\section{Proposed HEDO-HVSC Architecture}
\label{sec:methodology}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{!}{0.6\textheight}{\HEDOArchitectureFigure}
\caption{HEDO-HVSC pipeline. Frozen ViT-B/16 ($L_I=197$) and RoBERTa-base ($L_T=64$) features are projected to $d_{\text{model}}=128$, transformed by HEDO, and processed by chunk-wise SSD recurrence ($C=16$, $d_{\text{state}}=64$). During training, boundary states parameterize Gaussian latents regularized by $\mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}$ alongside multi-positive InfoNCE; at test time, deterministic posterior means are computed strictly unimodally.}
\label{fig:overall_architecture}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Backbone features.} As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:overall_architecture}, an image $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$ and a caption $\mathbf{T}$ are encoded by frozen backbones and linearly projected:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_I^{(0)} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}, I} \, \text{ViT-B/16}(\mathbf{I}), \qquad \mathbf{X}_T^{(0)} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{proj}, T} \, \text{RoBERTa}(\mathbf{T}), \label{eq:backbone}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{X}_I^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L_I \times d_{\text{model}}}$ ($L_I = 197$: 196 patches plus a class token), $\mathbf{X}_T^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L_T \times d_{\text{model}}}$, and a text mask $\mathbf{M}_T \in \{0, 1\}^{B \times L_T}$. Both backbones remain frozen in all experiments.

\subsection{Hamiltonian-Inspired Energy Dissipation Operator (HEDO)}
Given input coordinates $\mathbf{q}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times L \times d_{\text{model}}}$, HEDO initializes momenta $\mathbf{p}_0 = \tanh(\mathbf{W}_p \mathbf{q}_0)$ and applies a damped coordinate-momentum update with $\Delta t = 0.1$, $\beta = 0.05$, and $\gamma = 0.1$:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{p}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{p}_t (1 - \beta \Delta t) - \Delta t \tanh(\mathbf{W}_q \mathbf{q}_t), \label{eq:momentum_update} \\
\mathbf{q}_{t+1} &= \mathbf{q}_t + \gamma \Delta t \, \mathbf{p}_{t+1}, \qquad \text{HEDO}(\mathbf{q}) = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{q}_{t+1}), \label{eq:hedo}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_p, \mathbf{W}_q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$ are learnable. With the empirical energy $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{p}) = \frac{1}{2}(\|\mathbf{q}\|_2^2 + \|\mathbf{p}\|_2^2)$, update \eqref{eq:momentum_update} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf{p}_{t+1}\|_2 \le (1 - \beta \Delta t)\|\mathbf{p}_t\|_2 + \Delta t \|\tanh(\mathbf{W}_q \mathbf{q}_t)\|_2 .
\end{equation}
Since $\|\tanh(\cdot)\|_2 \le \sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}$, the contraction factor $(1 - \beta \Delta t) = 0.995$ limits momentum amplification under the bounded force term. In pre-training diagnostics, HEDO reduced image representation energy from $\mathcal{H}_{\text{raw}} = 170.824$ to $\mathcal{H}_{\text{HEDO}} = 77.563$ while preserving cosine similarity $0.9952$; however, unforced multi-step trajectories were not monotonically decreasing (images: $170.824 \to 174.387$ over six steps; text: $5.849 \to 5.892$). We therefore describe HEDO as a damping inductive bias, not a system with guaranteed monotonic energy decay.

\subsection{State-Continuous Chunk-Wise SSD-Style Recurrence}
The HEDO output $\mathbf{X}$ is projected to $d_{\text{state}} = 64$ by $\mathbf{B}_{\text{proj}}$, with learned decay $\mathbf{A}_{\text{decay}} = \exp(-\exp(\mathbf{A}_{\text{log}})) \in (0, 1)^{d_{\text{state}}}$. The sequence is split into $K = \lceil L / C \rceil$ chunks of size $C = 16$, and hidden states evolve as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{t} = m_t \left( \mathbf{A}_{\text{decay}} \odot \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{proj}} \mathbf{x}_t \right) + (1 - m_t) \mathbf{h}_{t-1},
\end{equation}
where $m_t \in \{0, 1\}$ marks valid tokens, so padded positions preserve the state. States carry across chunks ($\mathbf{h}_{\text{start}, k} = \mathbf{h}_{\text{end}, k-1}$, $\mathbf{h}_{\text{start}, 1} = \mathbf{0}$); the final state of each chunk is recorded as boundary state $\mathbf{b}_k$ with validity flag $m_k = \mathbb{I}(\sum_{t \in k} m_t > 0)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:chunk_recurrence}).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{0.9\textwidth}{!}{\HEDOChunkFigure}
\caption{State-continuous chunk-wise recurrence with boundary state extraction. Hidden states propagate across chunk boundaries without resets, and mask-weighted aggregation prevents padding bias.}
\label{fig:chunk_recurrence}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Chunk-Wise Variational State Coupling (HVSC)}
HVSC aggregates valid boundary states and parameterizes a diagonal Gaussian with $z_{\text{dim}} = 64$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K m_k \mathbf{b}_k}{\sum_{k=1}^K m_k + \epsilon}, \label{eq:hbound}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\mu} = \mathbf{W}_\mu \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}}, \qquad \log \boldsymbol{\sigma}^2 = \text{clamp}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{logvar}} \mathbf{h}_{\text{bound}}, -5, 2), \label{eq:latent}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$. During training, latents are sampled as $\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \odot \boldsymbol{\sigma}$ with $\boldsymbol{\epsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})$. The sequence is mean-pooled (mask-weighted for text) into $\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}}$ and fused as $\mathbf{e} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}} \mathbf{z})$ with $\alpha = 0.1$. Cross-modal boundary alignment is regularized in FP32 by the symmetric KL divergence
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}(p_I \parallel p_T) = \tfrac{1}{2} \left[ D_{\text{KL}}(p_I \parallel p_T) + D_{\text{KL}}(p_T \parallel p_I) \right], \label{eq:skl}
\end{equation}
computed analytically for diagonal Gaussians. At test time, sampling is omitted and the posterior means are used:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{e}}_M = \text{Normalize}\big(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pool}, M} + \alpha \mathbf{W}_{\text{out}} \boldsymbol{\mu}_M)\big), \qquad M \in \{I, T\}.
\end{equation}
Visual and textual embeddings are thus computed independently, with no cross-modal attention or hidden-state exchange.

\subsection{Training Objective}
The loss is $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} + \lambda_{\text{KL}} \mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}$ with $\lambda_{\text{KL}} = 0.01$, where the multi-positive InfoNCE accounts for the 5 captions per image:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ \log \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}(i)} e^{s_{i,j} / \tau}}{\sum_{k=1}^{5B} e^{s_{i,k} / \tau}} + \log \frac{e^{s_{i,i} / \tau}}{\sum_{k=1}^B e^{s_{k,i} / \tau}} \right], \label{eq:infonce}
\end{equation}
where $s_{i,j} = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_{I, i}^\top \hat{\mathbf{e}}_{T, j}$, $\mathcal{P}(i)$ is the positive caption set of image $i$, and the learnable logit scale is capped at $100$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:hedo_hvsc} summarizes one training step.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{HEDO-HVSC forward pass and training step}
\label{alg:hedo_hvsc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Images $\mathbf{I} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times 224 \times 224}$, tokens $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{Z}^{B \times 64}$, masks $\mathbf{M}_T$, positive index sets $\mathcal{P}$
\Ensure Total loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$, test embeddings $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_I, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_T$
\State Extract frozen features $\mathbf{X}_I^{(0)}, \mathbf{X}_T^{(0)}$ via \eqref{eq:backbone}
\State Apply the HEDO transformation, if enabled, via \eqref{eq:momentum_update}--\eqref{eq:hedo}
\State Run state-continuous chunk-wise SSD recurrence; collect boundary states $\mathbf{b}_k$ and flags $m_k$
\State Aggregate boundary states and parameterize latents via \eqref{eq:hbound}--\eqref{eq:latent}
\State Compute $\mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}(p_I \parallel p_T)$ in FP32 via \eqref{eq:skl}
\State Sample $\mathbf{z}$ and form coupled embeddings $\mathbf{e}_I, \mathbf{e}_T$
\State Compute $\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}}$ via \eqref{eq:infonce}
\State \Return $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} \gets \mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} + 0.01 \cdot \mathcal{D}_{\text{SKL}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\textbf{Dataset.} We use Flickr8k~\cite{hodosh2013framing}: 8,000 images with 5 captions each, split into 6,000 / 1,000 / 1,000 images (30,000 / 5,000 / 5,000 captions) for training, checkpoint selection, and held-out testing. Disjointness of the splits was verified via image identifier hashing.

\textbf{Backbones and implementation.} The visual backbone is the \texttt{timm} checkpoint \texttt{vit\_base\_}\allowbreak\texttt{patch16\_224} (86.6\,M parameters, ImageNet normalization) and the text backbone is \texttt{roberta-base} (124.6\,M parameters, $L_T=64$); both are frozen, and only projections, HEDO, SSD, and HVSC modules (0.330--0.422\,M parameters) are trained. We use Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.10.0 (CUDA 12.8), \texttt{timm} 1.0.26, and \texttt{transformers} 5.0.0 on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 (16\,GB), with AMP FP16 and gradient scaling. Checkpoints are selected by peak validation Mean Recall (patience 3 epochs, $\Delta_{\text{min}}=0.05\%$), and the test split is evaluated once. Table~\ref{tab:hyperparameters} lists the hyperparameters.

\textbf{Protocol and metrics.} Four configurations are evaluated with three seeds each (12 runs): (i) Custom SSD-Style Baseline, (ii) w/o HEDO, (iii) w/o HVSC, and (iv) Full HEDO-HVSC. Image-to-text (I2T) R@K ranks all 5,000 test captions per image and succeeds if any ground-truth caption is in the top $K$; text-to-image (T2I) R@K ranks all 1,000 test images per caption. Mean Recall (MR) averages the six recalls. For robustness, a 100-image (500-caption) test subset was fixed a priori with seed 42 and evaluated under identical corruptions for all models; Gaussian noise was added pixel-wise with a fixed seed before clipping to $[0, 1]$.

\textbf{Robustness.} To examine whether dissipation attenuates perturbations, the baseline and full model were evaluated on the fixed 100-image subset under pixel-space corruptions (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}, Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}). The profile is mixed: the full model is $+0.50\%$ better under severe Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.10$) but lower on clean images ($-0.87\%$), mild noise ($-0.47\%$), and brightness increase ($-0.60\%$). This subset experiment is a secondary diagnostic rather than a comprehensive robustness evaluation.

\textbf{Scaling and footprint.} Latency was measured for $L \in \{16, \dots, 256\}$ ($d_{\text{model}}=128$, $d_{\text{state}}=64$) after 10 warm-up iterations, averaged over 50 timed iterations with explicit CUDA synchronization (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). A linear fit, $\text{Latency}(L) = 0.149 L + 0.582$\,ms ($R^2 = 0.9998$), characterizes the measured range; the $\mathcal{O}(L)$ complexity follows from the sequential recurrence itself. The full model has $0.422$\,M trainable parameters (projections 46.7\%, SSD blocks 23.3\%, HVSC 22.4\%, HEDO 7.8\%, normalization 0.2\%), under $0.21\%$ of the 209.854\,M frozen backbone parameters, with 2686.25\,MB peak training VRAM (baseline: 0.330\,M, 1878.22\,MB; w/o HEDO: 0.356\,M, 1878.81\,MB; w/o HVSC: 0.397\,M, 2685.66\,MB).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The full model matches the baseline on clean retrieval (paired delta $+0.03\% \pm 0.20\%$). Because only three seeds were evaluated per configuration, variability is reported descriptively rather than through inferential tests. The w/o HVSC configuration attains the highest Mean Recall, so the experiments do not show that HVSC improves clean-data accuracy; its role is best interpreted as a training-time cross-modal boundary-distribution regularizer, with mask weighting preventing padding from contaminating boundary states. HEDO yields a small advantage under severe Gaussian noise but not across all corruptions.

\textbf{What the results do not establish.} The results do not establish state-of-the-art accuracy over fine-tuned, unconstrained dual encoders; HEDO does not provide a monotonic Lyapunov stability guarantee for discrete learned dynamics; robustness is not universally superior; the PyTorch recurrence is not compared against native CUDA Mamba-2 kernels; and scaling is measured only for $L \in [16, 256]$.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} Evaluation is limited to Flickr8k; extending it to Flickr30k~\cite{young2014image} and MS-COCO~\cite{lin2014microsoft} is future work. Backbones were frozen, and joint fine-tuning may increase capacity. Compiling the recurrence into native CUDA kernels should further accelerate inference.

\textbf{Data and code availability.} Experiments use the public Flickr8k dataset. Source code, configurations, evaluation scripts, and benchmark manifests are publicly available; the repository link is withheld for double-blind review.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced HEDO-HVSC, a multimodal state-space framework combining a Hamiltonian-inspired discrete dissipative operator with mask-weighted chunk-wise variational state coupling. In a 12-run controlled factorial benchmark on Flickr8k, HEDO-HVSC achieves retrieval performance comparable to a custom SSD-style baseline ($48.28\% \pm 0.59\%$ Mean Recall). Measured latency scales approximately linearly over the tested sequence lengths, consistent with the linear recurrent structure, and robustness evaluation shows a small advantage under severe Gaussian noise with mixed results under other perturbations.
